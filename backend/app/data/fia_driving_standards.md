# FIA Driving Standards & Protest Procedure — teaching corpus

This file is an **original educational paraphrase** of publicly discussed Formula 1
driving-standards and International Sporting Code *topics*.
It is **not** official FIA Sporting Regulations text and must not be cited as a real
Steward Decision, Protest, or Right of Review filing.

## Appendix L Chapter IV Article 2 — Causing a collision

Teaching paraphrase of ISC Appendix L, Chapter IV, Article 2 d): a driver must not
cause a collision. Contact that could reasonably have been avoided by the car that
closed the gap is typically assigned to that car. Understeer into a car that has
established overlap on the outside is a common pattern for a "causing a collision"
finding when the inside car fails to leave racing room.

## Appendix L Chapter IV — Leaving racing room at the apex

Apex ownership is judged by whether a car is alongside at the apex, not merely at
braking. The car entitled to racing room must still leave a car's width. Closing the
door when the other car is already alongside is treated as failing to leave room.
Conversely, a car that is not alongside at the apex does not automatically own the exit.

## Overtaking on the inside

A car attempting a pass on the inside is generally expected to be clearly alongside before the other car turns in.
Alongside is judged at the apex, not at braking. The overtaking car must leave a car’s width if it has earned the right to the corner.
A dive that only puts the front wing alongside at the apex, then forces the other car off, is typically treated as the overtaking driver causing the incident.

## Overtaking on the outside

A car that is fully alongside on the outside through the apex is generally entitled to racing room on the exit.
The defending car must not squeeze the outside car beyond the white line if the outside car is still alongside.
If the outside car is not alongside at the apex, the defending car may use the racing line; the outside car then owns the risk of running out of road.

## Defending and moving under braking

A driver may choose a defensive line once. More than one change of direction to block is typically treated as illegal weaving.
Moving in reaction after the other car has committed to a braking point is a common basis for a causing-a-collision finding.
Brake-test style deceleration with no racing purpose is treated as dangerous driving, not a legitimate defend.

## Leaving room and causing a collision

Both cars must leave a fair width when they are genuinely side-by-side.
Contact that could reasonably have been avoided by the car that closed the gap is usually assigned to that car.
If both close the gap, stewards often record racing incident with no further action.
Rear-end contact where the following car misjudges closing speed is typically the following car’s responsibility unless the car ahead braked erratically.

## Track limits

A lap time is generally deleted if a car leaves the track without a justifiable reason and gains a lasting advantage.
Repeated track-limits warnings can escalate to a black-and-white flag and then a time penalty.
Leaving the track to complete an overtake, then failing to give the position back, is treated as gaining a lasting advantage.

## Rejoining and unsafe return

A car that leaves the track must rejoin without forcing another car to take evasive action.
Rejoining across the racing line in the braking zone is a typical unsafe-rejoin pattern.

## Blue flags and lapped cars

A lapped car must allow the faster car through at the earliest safe opportunity once blue flags are shown.
A lapped car may not defend against the leaders. Blocking under blue flags is typically a time penalty.

## Safety car, VSC, and yellow flags

Overtaking is generally prohibited under safety car, VSC, and double-yellow conditions except as specifically allowed.
Slowing for yellows must be genuine; a driver who lifts less than peers in the same zone can be reviewed for failing to slow.

## Starting-grid and first-lap incidents

First-lap bunching is often recorded as a racing incident unless one car makes an avoidable move.
Jump starts are a separate start-procedure offence, usually a time penalty.
Opening-lap contact may attract a mitigated penalty when compressed racing conditions and loss of front-end grip are proven.

## Penalty ladder (typical public outcomes)

No further action / racing incident: contact with shared or unclear blame.
Reprimand: minor, avoidable but limited sporting effect.
Time penalty (5s / 10s): causing a collision, gaining a lasting advantage, or ignoring flags.
Drive-through or stop-go: more serious or repeat offences.

## ISC Article 13 — Protests

Teaching paraphrase: a Competitor may lodge a Protest against another Competitor or
against the results when they believe a breach of the regulations has occurred.
A Protest must identify the parties, the session, the alleged infringement, and the
evidence relied upon. Filings that lack specific regulatory citations or that cannot
point to reviewable evidence are routinely rejected as inadmissible. The protest
window and formality requirements are strict; late or informal complaints are not
treated as Protests.

## ISC Article 14 — Right of Review

Teaching paraphrase: a Right of Review may be requested when a party asserts that a
**significant and relevant new element** was unavailable to the stewards when they
took the original decision. The new element must be:
1. **Significant** — capable of changing the outcome or the attribution of fault.
2. **New** — not already before the stewards in the original hearing.
3. **Relevant** — connected to the incident facts, not a re-argument of the same video.

Whole-lap public telemetry or a second copy of the same broadcast angle typically
fails the "new and significant" test. High-frequency steering angle, brake pressure
at apex entry, throttle lift delta, and synchronized onboard T-Cam are the kinds of
elements teams argue qualify when they were not in the original evidence pack.

## Evidentiary standards for understeer and contact claims

To sustain a protest that Car A understeered into Car B, counsel typically needs:
- High-frequency steering-angle trace from the 100m board through apex.
- Brake pressure / longitudinal G at turn-in.
- Throttle lift timing relative to the other car.
- Onboard T-Cam or helmet-cam synchronized to the same timestamps.
- GPS / location samples dense enough to show lateral gap closure (not lap averages).

Coarse OpenF1 car_data (speed/brake/throttle at low sample rates) can support a
preliminary claim but is usually insufficient alone to prove understeer or contact.
